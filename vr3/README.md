# VR3 — Inbox Zero, four painted levels

A separate IWSDK web VR prototype. It began as one level, "Kyoto, after the rain": two matching 270° panoramas blended in one shader, with a little actual 3D motion in front. There are now four levels built the same way, and a home screen to choose between them (see "Levels and the home screen" below). No Unity runtime, imported environment models, physics, shadows, or locomotion. Most of this file was written about Kyoto and still describes it.

## Run

Use Node 24 (`nvm use` in this folder), then `npm install` and `npm run dev`. Open http://localhost:5183.

The page opens on the home screen: pick a level, then Start (Enter VR in a headset browser; on a monitor it plays on the screen). On a monitor: drag or use arrow keys to look around, X or Space uses the weapon, C keeps the letter, Esc goes back to the menu, H opens Settings (the lighting transition, Atmosphere, and the katana's hold). `?level=office` (or `castle`, `lab`, `kyoto`) opens on that level and `&play=1` skips the home screen.

For a headset on Wi-Fi, run `npm run dev:https`, open the network HTTPS URL printed by Vite, accept the local development certificate, then choose Enter VR. Alternatively use `adb reverse tcp:5183 tcp:5183` over USB and open http://localhost:5183 in the headset browser. HTTPS or localhost is required for immersive WebXR; the HTTP LAN address only supports desktop preview.

`npm run build` typechecks and produces `dist`. Deploy that folder to an HTTPS static host. `npm run preview` serves the build on port 4183.

## Levels and the home screen (2026-11-11)

**The menu in VR** (2026-11-11, "cut to select"; `showMenu()` in `src/letters.ts`, its events in `src/main.ts`). Where a game ends in a headset, so a player can choose and play again without taking it off. Out of black, with the level dimmed: the levels as cards in an arc at arm's reach (each with its home-screen picture and its name in pixels, the chosen one outlined in its colour with an OK), CLEAN YOUR INBOX under them in the chosen level's colour, a small EXIT VR off to the right, and the chosen level's logo floating above with a line saying what to do. Since 2026-11-12 the cards are in rows of four with COMING SOON blanks filling the last row (room for eight, as on the flat screen), and choosing a level is a gentle dip of the view (to about 40% dark) while it changes, the menu coming back in place, not a trip through black. The left hand chooses the levels and EXIT VR: its reach line or where it points lights a card (it swells and comes forward a little, a tick in the hand), and a squeeze takes it; the blade and the spray pass through those. Only CLEAN YOUR INBOX is slashed (or sprayed) to play. A chosen level's card shatters, its name is said, the view goes to black while the level changes, and the menu comes again around the new level; START begins a game; EXIT VR leaves VR for the home screen. Entering VR from the home screen still goes straight into a game. Checked in the headset emulator (end of a game to the menu, a level picked, start, exit); not yet by a real swing on the Quest.

- **A level** is an entry in `src/levels/index.ts`: its paintings, its stage, its letters, its effects, its sound and its weapon. Kyoto is still its own files (`garden.ts`, `sword.ts`); the others are one file each in `src/levels` plus a weapon in `src/weapons`. - **The stage** (`src/levels/backdrop.ts`) is Kyoto's idea made general: two paintings (start and end lighting, from `design/theme-tests-v2`, copied to `public/levels/<id>`) on a sphere that follows the head, the same 270° by 90° wrap without Kyoto's squeeze, and past the painting's edges the edge itself blurred right out so floor and ceiling carry on. A level supplies a few lines of GLSL: `inverseDepthAt()` (how far away each direction is, for stereo and leaning, a model of the scene as in Kyoto) and `scene()` (its moving lights and weather). - **The letters** take a theme (`LetterTheme` in `src/letters.ts`): a painted face instead of ink on washi (`src/levels/cards.ts`), a shape of their own (`src/levels/shapes.ts`; everything that holds, hits and cuts a letter works on any of them), where they come from, their colours, how what is left of one goes, and whether they are cut or sprayed. Kyoto's theme is the letters exactly as they were.
- **A held weapon** is a model and a `HeldSpec` (`src/weapons/held.ts`): how it sits in the fist, the two ends of what hits, or its nozzle. The same file puts it in the right hand, stages it on a monitor, draws its swing ribbon and its glow while the trigger makes it live. `weapon.html?w=keyboard` (or `knight`, `sprayer`) is a viewer for modelling one against its reference sheet (development only).

## Scene

- A 45 m inward sphere wraps a purpose-built seated panorama across 270°. The whole courtyard occupies the center of the artwork, with continuous covered veranda at either side. A smooth horizontal remap brings the courtyard into the forward view without introducing a panel boundary. The rear gap and vertical poles fade into shelter color.
- The backdrop follows viewer translation; head rotation remains tracked. A softly weighted, head-translation-driven relief offset shifts the near veranda more than the distant sky. The displacement is clamped to 0.002 UV, with 18 cm of supported lean and a Depth strength control. It captures a fresh seated origin after VR entry and freezes with Motion off. This is subtle 2.5D relief, not reconstructed geometry or walkable space; no autonomous camera motion or change to the approved central framing.
- 72 instanced six-triangle cherry petals and 12 leaves tumble slowly. Light detail uses 32 petals and 5 leaves. These are tiny procedural meshes; external model packages are unnecessary.
- 120 dust points (48 on light detail), a hemisphere light, and a warm lantern light add atmosphere.
- 28 softly glowing, slowly drifting light motes (12 on light detail) add depth. Layered ground mist, two different speeds of drifting cloud haze plus masked movement of the painted sky, and warm lamp/reflection halos are calculated in the panorama shader, using no extra textures or fullscreen bloom buffers. Lantern illumination and halos share a gentle flicker. The Motion toggle also freezes these effects.
- Softly masked puddle distortion animates the open water and reflections with sideways-travelling shallow waves and analytic ripple-slope shading (a lightweight normal-map approximation). Both time-of-day images use the same sampling offset. It avoids the central stone path and veranda, responds to Breeze, freezes with Motion off, and adds no textures or draw calls.
- A tiny masked texture-coordinate distortion moves the cherry canopy. Both images receive identical distortion. This approximates wind; it is not an articulated tree.
- No floating HUD or player mesh in immersive mode. Set mood and motion using browser controls before entering.

## The game's core (2026-11-11)

Emails arrive one at a time as paper letters, and there are two things to do with one. This is the core of the first level only, for getting the feel right: no score, no rounds, no end.

- **The letter** is the Unity game's own model (a slightly curled 50 by 31 cm sheet; `tools/letter.py` brings it and its washi texture over to `public/mail`). Each email is drawn by that game's own card code, copied word for word into `src/mail` (see its README), and shown as ink on the paper by the material in `src/letters.ts`.
- **Start.** One letter is waiting: "INBOX ZERO, cut this one to begin". Cutting it or throwing it starts the emails, shuffled from the game's made-up inbox, without end.
- **A letter's path.** Out of the garden to 1.5 m out by the veranda's edge, about ten degrees below eye level, where a seated player's eyes naturally rest; then it keeps coming for seven seconds, growing, down to 0.8 m and the height of the hands; then, not dealt with, it goes past the shoulder and the next one comes.
- **Cut it** with the katana, at any point of that path. The blade's sweep between frames is tested against a box round the sheet (12 cm deep, 5 cm past its edges, 0.9 m/s least speed), and the sheet is cut in two along the line the blade was travelling, through where it met the paper (`src/cut.ts` cuts the mesh itself, so the halves are the real halves, ink and all). The pieces hang and turn in the air and can be cut again and again, until the next letter is in focus; then they burn away. A cut gives a streak of light along it, a glowing edge on the paper, sparks, petals and shreds, the slash sound, a moment's hit-stop and a buzz in the right hand.
- **The sword's long reach.** Past its tip the blade goes on as an unseen line for another 1.3 m, its hit box widening as it goes, so a swing cuts a letter that is still small and far. It counts only once the letter has arrived in its place (not on its way in from the garden), and only while the real tip is moving at 2 m/s or more, so a resting wrist cannot cut something two metres off. Pieces can be cut along it too.
- **Hard cuts shatter** (2026-11-11, `shatter` at the top of `src/letters.ts`). A gentle swing still cuts a letter cleanly in two. Past about 3 m/s at the blade each half breaks again (up to four pieces), past about 5.3 m/s again (up to eight), along lines roughly across the first cut; the pieces fly apart harder, out from where the blade went in, and spin faster; the level throws out its own fragments (mint pixel squares off an office pane, parchment and wood splinters off a dispatch, washi and petals in Kyoto) and more of its light; and the hit's moment of stillness lasts a little longer. No more breaking once 20 pieces are in the air. Pieces can be hit for as long as they are in the air, even while they burn away (they used to stop being cuttable when they were small, once 16 were in the air, or once burning had begun); a piece too small to cut again, or hit when 24 are already up, bursts into fragments instead, and so does one the cut's line happens to miss, so every hit on a piece does something. Pieces also have a slightly larger hit box than a letter, being smaller and moving.
- **Keep it** with the left hand. The hand has a long reach as well, the way the hands do in Needle VR (`../../needlevr/src/reach.ts`): its hit box is a line from the hand on outwards, away from the shoulder, 1.7 m long and widening, so reaching roughly towards a letter is enough (within about 25 degrees); pointing the controller at it works too, more narrowly. A tick in the hand, a warm edge on the paper and a thread of light from hand to letter say it has been found. Close grip or trigger and it flies in, stopping 45 cm short of the hand and shrinking to about six tenths of its size, so that it does not fill the view; then throw it in any direction and let go, or just sweep it away fast and it leaves by itself at the throw's fastest point. A thrown letter is marked as one that matters: it flashes as it leaves the hand, with a ring of sparks, and wears a gold edge that beats while it is in the air. It leaves at one and a half times the hand's speed, at once, and from there is paper in the air: it falls, slows and tumbles as paper does, and can be cut all the way. After a second and a half of flight, or when it lands, or gets 8 m off, it turns to warm light and is gone in a breath of sparks. Only then is it kept: cut out of the air first, it is just a cut letter, and the gold goes. Let go of gently, it lifts away. A grip still closed from the last throw does not take the next letter: it has to close again.
- **Trigger cuts.** With a controller the sword cuts only while its trigger is held, so it can be waved about without cutting anything; a blade that is not cutting sheds no petals or sparks either. Making it live is felt, heard and seen: a tick in the hand; the sound of a knife drawn; (a quiet hum while the trigger was held, rising with the swing, was taken out on 2026-11-11: HUM_LEVEL 0 in src/ambience.ts); and the blade glowing, its steel lit from within and a soft light running its length (`src/liveblade.ts`, which dresses the katana from outside). The knife and the hum are ElevenLabs takes prepared by `tools/swordsounds.py`. This is an idea being tried: the Sword controls' Trigger cuts box (remembered on the device) or `?triggerCut=0` turns it off.
- **Controllers first.** All of this is tuned for Quest controllers. Bare hands will want settings of their own (there is no trigger, for one); that is a second set for later, chosen by what the player is holding.
- **On a monitor** there are no hands: **X** cuts the letter in front of you, **C** throws it, and Preview sword swing swings the real blade through it.
- Every number worth trying on the headset is in `tune` at the top of `src/letters.ts`, and live at `__vr3.letters.tune` in a development build.

The Sword controls now default to what felt good on the headset with controllers on 2026-11-11: a 58 cm blade, a 3 cm hand gap and a 28 degree lean, with the blade turned 15 degrees to the left (a new control, Turn left: with none, the blade points about 7 degrees to the right of straight ahead of a level controller; with 15, about 10 to the left). The blade slider goes down to 43 cm.

Verified in desktop Chromium and the headset emulator, by script: with Trigger cuts on, a swing cuts only with the trigger held; a letter taken from afar is held 40 cm from the hand at 0.62 of its size; a thrown one leaves at 4.1 m/s from a 2.9 m/s sweep, rises, falls and is kept as it lands 1.9 s later; the title is cut by a real blade sweep and starts the game; pieces survive the next letter's arrival and burn once it is in focus; a letter 1.5 m away is taken by reaching towards it (on within 20 cm of true aim at arm's length, off with the hand in the lap or at the side) or by pointing at it from the lap, flies in to the hand, and is thrown at about 3 m/s; a swing cuts a letter a metre beyond the blade's tip, and a slow drift of the sword across it does not; one let go of drifts off; one left alone goes past after its approach; production build passes. Not verified: any of it on a headset. Distances, speeds and how forgiving the blade and the hand are were set by reasoning about a seated player, not by feel.

## Sakura katana

`src/sword.ts` builds a lightweight Three.js katana from curved beveled blade geometry, crossed rose cloth ribbons over ivory rayskin, burgundy fittings, and an antique-brass blossom guard. Three 256px procedural textures and a small generated reflection environment supply surface detail without downloaded models or postprocessing. The design reference is `design/sword/reference-sheet.png`.

In VR the handle follows the right input source's native grip pose, with no artificial swing animation. The `hold` matrix at the top of `src/sword.ts` places it for seated play: blade out of the thumb side, cutting edge towards the knuckles, the fist right under the guard (index finger against it), a 24° forward lean, and a blade stretched to 90 cm so the reach comes from the sword and not from a gap. The Sword section of the controls sets Hand gap (fist to guard), Lean and Blade length, with a reset; they are remembered on the device and are set before entering VR. `?reach=0.045&tilt=24&blade=1.25` in the address bar overrides them. Hands, in the same section, draws the SDK's outline hands where the controllers are held, the free hand's fingers following trigger and grip; the sword hand stays a closed fist turned to the sword's lean, because left to the buttons it hangs open beside the hilt whenever the fingers relax (`src/hands.ts`, which also works around two faults in IWSDK 1.1.0-rc.2 that stop that hand loading; the hand model comes from the jsDelivr input-profiles CDN). Controller meshes and rays stay hidden. Loss of tracking hides the sword and clears the trail. A pooled, velocity-driven lavender ribbon fades over 180ms; discontinuous tracking and session changes reset it. Motion off disables the ribbon while retaining controller tracking.
